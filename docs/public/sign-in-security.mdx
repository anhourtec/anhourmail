---
title: Sign-in security
description: Your AnhourMail console sign-in is your password first, then a passkey or authenticator app code if you've set one up; see where you're signed in.
---

You always sign in to the AnhourMail console with your email and password first. If you've added a passkey or an
authenticator app, the console then asks for a second step. You set these up on your **Security** page: open the menu
under your initials in the top-right corner and choose **Security**.

These settings protect the console. Mail apps (phone, desktop) keep signing in with app passwords, as before.

## Choose your own password at first sign-in

If an admin [added you as a user](/docs/mailboxes#add-a-user) and asked you to change your password, the console
shows **Choose your own password** before anything else. Enter the **password you were given**, then a new one twice,
and choose **Save and continue**. This changes your console password; your mailbox keeps its own, and the
[webmail](/docs/webmail#sign-in) asks you to replace that one the first time you sign in there.

## Passwords seen in data breaches

When you choose a new password — signing up, resetting it, joining an account from an invite, or setting a mailbox
password — we check that it hasn't appeared in a known data breach. If it has, you'll see "<Value of="breachedPasswordMessage" />"
Pick another one: a passphrase of a few unrelated words, or a generated
password saved in your password manager, works well.

The check never sends your password anywhere. Our server turns it into a one-way hash (SHA-1) and sends only the first
5 characters of that hash to the [Pwned Passwords](https://haveibeenpwned.com/Passwords) service, which answers with
every breached hash starting with them — hundreds, padded to a similar size. Our server compares the rest itself, so
the service never learns your password, its full hash, or who you are. Your browser doesn't contact the service at
all. If it can't be reached in time, your password is accepted as usual.

## Two-factor sign-in with an authenticator app

After your password, the console asks for a <Value of="totpDigits" />-digit code from an authenticator app on your
phone or computer. Any standard app works (it uses the common time-based codes).

To turn it on:

1. On your Security page, under **Authenticator app**, choose **Turn on** and confirm your password.
2. Scan the QR code with your app. If you can't scan it, add an account by hand with the set-up key shown below the
   code. The QR code is drawn in your browser; the key never goes to another service.
3. Enter the code your app shows and choose **Turn on**.
4. Save your recovery codes (below). They're shown once.

Each code works once: if you sign in twice within the same 30 seconds, wait for the next code.

To turn it off, choose **Turn off** and enter your password and a current code (or a recovery code).

## The second step

After your password, the console shows **Two-step sign-in** with the way you set up: **Use your passkey** if you have a
passkey, or a box for the code from your authenticator app. If you have both, **Use your passkey instead** and **Use
your authenticator app instead** switch between them. **Start again** goes back to the password.

## Recovery codes

When you add your first second step — a passkey or an authenticator app — you get <Value of="recoveryCodeCount" />
recovery codes. If you lose your passkey or authenticator app, choose **Use a recovery code instead** on the second
step and type one of them. Each code works once.

- Keep them somewhere safe, such as a password manager. **Copy** and **Download** help you save them.
- We store only a scrambled (hashed) form of each code, so we can't show them to you again.
- Your Security page shows how many are left and warns you when <Value of="recoveryCodeWarnAt" /> or fewer remain.
  **New recovery codes** gives you a fresh set and stops the old ones working.
- If your second step is a passkey, your codes are under **Recovery codes** on your Security page. If you added a
  passkey before recovery codes came with it, choose **Create recovery codes** there.
- Turning on an authenticator app gives you a new set once it's set up. Turning it off while you keep a passkey keeps
  your codes.

## Trust this browser

On the second step — passkey or code — you can tick **Trust this browser for <Value of="trustedDeviceDays" /> days**.
That browser then skips the second step until the time runs out (unless your account requires a passkey: then the
passkey is always asked). **Sign out everywhere else** on your Security page makes every browser ask
again, and so do turning two-factor sign-in off and resetting your password. Resetting your password also signs you out
everywhere.

Passkeys keep working after a password reset. Afterwards your Security page asks you to check them and remove any you
don't recognise. If you think someone else got into your login, tick **Also remove all passkeys** when you reset your
password, or when you choose **Sign out everywhere else** (which then asks for your console password). When every
passkey is removed, we email you and the Owners of your accounts, and it shows in those accounts' audit logs. If your account requires passkeys, they can't be removed
this way: ask an Owner of the account for help.

## Passkeys and security keys

A passkey is a second step after your password: your fingerprint, face, device PIN or a security key confirms it's
you. It's the strongest protection against phishing: it only works on the real AnhourMail console address. A passkey
never signs you in on its own; the password always comes first.

- **Add a passkey** on your Security page, give it a name (for example "Work laptop"), and follow your browser's
  prompts. If it's your first second step, save the recovery codes shown next. If you signed in more than a few minutes ago, you're asked for your console password first. We email you
  whenever a passkey is added, so you'd know if someone else did it.
- **Rename** or **Remove** a passkey from the list. Removing it here stops it working as your second step. Removing
  a passkey asks for your console password if you signed in more than a few minutes ago. When your last second step
  goes, trusted browsers are forgotten too. When your last passkey goes, we email you and the Owners of your accounts.
- To use it, sign in with your email and password, then choose **Use your passkey**.
- **Lost your passkey?** Use a recovery code (or your authenticator app) on the second step. Without any recovery
  codes, choose **Lost your passkey? Reset your password** on the second step, and tick **Also remove all passkeys**
  when you set the new password.

Your passkey has to check that it's you (PIN, fingerprint or face). Security keys without a PIN set can't be added.

## Confirm it's you

Some actions open mail or change who can get in — making an app password, setting a mailbox password, adding or
removing a passkey, giving someone access to a mailbox, exporting or deleting data, and for Anhourtec staff, suspensions and
support access. If you signed in more than a few minutes ago, the console asks **Confirm it's you** first: enter your
**console password**. It's the same however you signed in. After several wrong passwords you're signed out.

## Wrong codes

After several wrong codes or passkey attempts the sign-in attempt ends and you start again. If wrong codes keep coming, sign-in with a
second step is locked for <Value of="secondStepLockMinutes" /> minutes. Every refused attempt shows in your recent
activity.

## Where you're signed in

**Where you're signed in** lists your console sessions with the browser, the system and the network part of the
address (like `203.0.113.x`), when each one signed in and when it was last active. **Sign out** ends one session at
once; **Sign out everywhere else** ends all but the one you're using.

## Recent activity

Your Security page lists your recent sign-ins (including failed and refused ones, and how you signed in) and every
change to how you sign in, newest first. It never shows codes, passwords or full addresses. Anything Anhourtec staff
did is labelled as such.

## Requiring it for everyone in an account

Owners can require a second step for everyone in their account, in the **Sign-in requirements** section of their
Security page:

- **Two-factor sign-in**: a second step after the password (authenticator app or recovery code), or a passkey. The
  password alone no longer signs in.
- **A passkey or security key**: after the password, people use their passkey as the second step.

Before you can require something, you need it yourself, and you need to have signed in that way (for a passkey
requirement: with your passkey as the second step). When someone sets up their first way to sign in because of the requirement, they and the account's Owners get
an email, so an unexpected change stands out. Anyone in the account who doesn't have it yet is taken to their
Security page after signing in, and the rest of the console opens once they've set it up. The change is recorded in the
account's [audit log](/docs/audit-log). Removing your last passkey or turning off your authenticator app is refused
while a requirement depends on it.
