## Aliases

An alias is another address that delivers to the same mailbox. Use one for work enquiries, another for family,
and reply from whichever address the mail came to.

To add one, open **Aliases** in the console and choose **Add an alias**. In the panel, choose **Choose an address**, type the part before the `@`, pick one
of your verified domains and the mailbox it delivers to, then select **Create alias**. It starts working within a
few seconds. An address can only be used once: a mailbox and an alias can't share it.

## Masked aliases

A masked alias is a random address, like `k7mq2xwv9tbn@yourdomain.ca`, that you give to one shop, newsletter or
sign-up form. If it starts getting spam, switch it off and the mail stops; you'll also know who shared it.

To make one, choose **Masked**, pick the mailbox it delivers to, and optionally add a note to remind you where you
used it. The new address is shown with a copy button. Masked aliases use the domain of the mailbox they deliver to.

## Turning aliases off and deleting them

Each alias has a switch in the list. While it's off, mail sent to it is refused and you can't send from it, but the address
stays yours, so you can switch it back on later. Deleting an alias (⋮ menu beside it, then **Delete…**) frees the address. Tick several aliases to turn them on or
off, or delete them, together.

If an owner or someone who manages users turns off an alias of your mailbox, it shows **Turned off by an
administrator**. You can still see it, but you can't turn it back on or delete it yourself. Ask them: when they turn
it back on, you can manage it again as usual.

When its mailbox is [suspended or deleted](/docs/mailboxes), an alias stops receiving mail too, and new aliases can't
point at a deleted mailbox. A deleted mailbox's aliases stay reserved while it can be restored, and are permanently
deleted with it.

Owners and people who manage users can manage aliases for every mailbox in the account. Everyone else sees and
manages the aliases of their own mailboxes. Notes are only visible inside your account.

Role addresses such as `postmaster@`, `abuse@`, `admin@`, `hostmaster@` and `webmaster@` can only be added by an
owner or someone who manages users. Whoever holds them receives the domain's abuse reports and the emails that
certificate authorities send to prove you own the domain.

## Catch-all

On paid plans with your own domain, a catch-all delivers mail sent to any address at the domain that doesn't exist
to one mailbox. It's off unless you turn it on, because it also catches spam sent to made-up names. See
[Catch-all](/docs/custom-domain/catch-all).
