---
title: App passwords
description: Create a password for each mail app (phone, desktop, printer), see it once, and revoke it when you lose the device.
---

Mail apps on your phone or computer don't sign in with your console password. They use an **app password**: a long
random password made for one app, that only works for your mailbox. If you lose a device, revoke its app password and
nothing else changes.

## Create one

1. In the console, open **App passwords** (under **Mail** in the left navigation). You see each mailbox that belongs to
   you.
2. Choose **Create app password** under the mailbox, and give it a name you'll recognise later, like "Phone" or "Work
   laptop".
3. Choose what it can be used for (see below), then **Create password**.
4. If you haven't signed in or confirmed it's you in the last <Value of="stepUpMinutes" /> minutes, the console asks for
   your console password, a code from your authenticator app or a recovery code first. An app password opens your mail,
   so we check it's really you.
5. The password is shown **once**. Copy it into your mail app straight away, with your full email address as the
   username. The spaces in it are only there to make it easier to read: type it without them.

We email you whenever an app password is created, so you'd notice one you didn't make.

<Callout title="We can't show it again">
  We never keep a copy of the password, and neither can our staff see it. If you lose it, revoke it and create a new
  one.
</Callout>

Each mailbox can have up to <Value of="appPasswordsPerMailbox" /> app passwords. Revoke ones you no longer use to make
room.

## What it can be used for

Tick only what the app needs. Anything you leave out is refused by the mail server for that password.

| Choice                 | What it allows                                                                   |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Read mail (IMAP)       | Mail apps that keep your folders in sync. Most apps use this.                    |
| Download mail (POP3)   | Older apps that download mail to one device. Off unless you tick it.             |
| Send mail (SMTP)       | Sending from the app. A printer or scanner that only sends needs just this one.  |
| Mail over HTTPS (JMAP) | JMAP mail apps: reading and sending mail over HTTPS. Off unless you tick it.     |
| Contacts and calendars | Contacts and calendar apps (CardDAV, CalDAV). They can't read your mail with it. |

App passwords never sign in to the console or to the webmail, and they can't change your mailbox's password,
filters or sharing, create other passwords, or look up other people on the service.

## Set up your mail app

Most apps fill in the server settings when you type your email address. If yours asks, use:

<ClientSettingsTable />

- **Username:** your full email address.
- **Password:** the app password you just created.

For contacts, see [Contacts](/docs/contacts). More help: [Mail app settings](/docs/clients/imap-smtp-settings).

## Revoke one

On the **App passwords** page, choose **Revoke** next to it and confirm. It shows as **Revoking** for a moment, then
disappears; the app using it stops signing in within a minute. **Revoke all app passwords** does the same for every one
on that mailbox at once.

People who manage mailboxes in your account (an Owner, or a User Administrator for Members' mailboxes) can also see the
names of a mailbox's app passwords and revoke them from the mailbox's **App passwords** tab. Nobody but you can create
one for your mailbox, and nobody can see a password after it's made.

## When a mailbox is suspended or deleted, or its person leaves

App passwords don't work while their mailbox is suspended, and work again if it's resumed. A deleted mailbox's app
passwords stop working at once and are removed with it. You can't create new ones for a suspended mailbox or one that's
being deleted. If the person a mailbox belongs to is removed from the account, the app passwords on that mailbox are
revoked.
