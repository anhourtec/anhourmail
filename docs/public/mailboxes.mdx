A mailbox is an address with its own storage, like `jane@example.org`. Every account can have an address at our
domain, <Value of="productDomain" /> (see [Your free address](/docs/free-address)). On the Plus and Supporter plans
you can also create mailboxes on any domain you've connected and verified (see
[Use your own domain](/docs/custom-domain)).

## Add a user

On the Plus and Supporter plans, **Add a user** creates a person and their mailbox in one go: they sign in to the
[webmail](/docs/webmail) and to the console with their new address, no invite email needed. Open **Mailboxes** and
choose **Add a user**. It takes four steps; **Back** and **Next** move between them, and you can reopen a step you've
already seen.

1. **Basics.** First and last name, and a **display name** (the name people see on their mail; it fills in from the
   first and last name until you change it). The **username** and domain make their address. **Storage** starts at an
   even share of the unassigned storage. **Automatically create a password** is on: we create a strong one when you
   finish and show it to you once. Turn it off to type your own (same rules as below). **Require this user to change
   their password when they first sign in** is on: the webmail and the console ask them for a new password before
   anything else. Until they change it, the password you gave them also works in mail apps.
2. **Role.** What they can do in the console. **Member** (the default) uses their own mailbox, aliases and app
   passwords, with no account settings. Owners can give any role; User Administrators add Members.
3. **Optional settings.** Job title, department, office, phone numbers and a postal address, all optional. They're
   kept with the person for your records and removed with them.
4. **Review and finish.** Check each part (**Edit** goes back to it), then choose **Finish adding**. Copy the password
   from the last screen and give it to them safely; it isn't shown again.

The new user also appears under [People and roles](/docs/people-and-roles). If they already have an AnhourMail login
with another address, [invite them](/docs/people-and-roles#invite-someone) instead.

When the account has no room for another user, **Add a user** is greyed out and says why: the account already has
all its people (counting pending invites), all the mailboxes its plan includes, or all of its storage is assigned
(lower another mailbox's storage first). Users need an address on a domain you've verified, or on our domain while
your plan has room for one; for our domain, confirm your own email address first.

### Make a shared mailbox a user

A mailbox that doesn't belong to anyone, like a [shared mailbox](#add-a-shared-mailbox), can become a person's own
mailbox. Open it, and on its **Overview** choose **Make this a user** next to **Belongs to**. The steps
are the same as **Add a user**, without the address and storage: their name and display name, a role, and the optional
profile details. Before finishing you confirm it's you with your console password or a code, because this replaces
the mailbox's password: we create a new one, show it to you once, and the person chooses their own the first time they
sign in to the webmail or the console. Anyone still using the old password is signed out of the webmail, and the
mailbox's [app passwords](/docs/clients/app-passwords) are revoked, so nobody who used the shared mailbox keeps a way
in. One exception: a mail app that is already connected with the old password (IMAP) stays connected until it
disconnects, so if the address was shared widely, assume the people who used it can still see new mail for a while.

Until the person has chosen their own password, the mailbox can't change its password with the old one, and a
"Forgot password" request for their console login sends no email (it would land in this mailbox). If they forget the
new password in the meantime, set it again from the **Password** tab.

If people have been [given access](#give-people-access-to-a-mailbox) to the mailbox, the last step lists them. They
keep their access, with one exception: when the new user gets a role other than **Member**, access that someone other
than an owner gave is taken away, because only an owner can give access to an owner's or an admin's mailbox.

## Add a shared mailbox

**Add a user** is the main way to add a mailbox. Use **Add a shared mailbox** for an address that isn't one person's
sign-in, like `support@` or `info@`, or for one more address for someone who's already in the account. It has its own
storage and password, and doesn't add anyone to People and roles. Once it's created, choose who can use it on its
**Delegation** tab (see [Give people access to a mailbox](#give-people-access-to-a-mailbox)).

1. In the console, open **Mailboxes**, open the **⋯** menu next to the search box and choose **Add a shared
   mailbox**. A panel opens on the right.
2. Type the part before the @ and choose the domain. Letters, numbers, dots, hyphens and underscores work;
   `+` is reserved, because `you+anything@` already reaches you. Our domain is in the list too, marked **free
   address**, while your plan has room for one; names there follow [their own rules](/docs/free-address#choosing-a-name).
3. Optionally, choose who it **belongs to**, or leave it for later.
4. Set its **storage** in whole GB. It starts at an even share of the account's unassigned storage, and can be up to
   all of it (see [Account storage](#account-storage)).
5. Set its **password**: type one, or choose **Generate a strong password** and copy it. A generated password is
   shown only until you create the mailbox. Give it to the person safely; they sign in to the
   [webmail](/docs/webmail) with it and can change it there.

Mailbox passwords need 12 to 128 characters (a passphrase works well), no spaces at the start or end, and can't be
the mailbox's address or start with `app_`. We never store a mailbox password: it's turned into a one-way hash right
away, and only the mail server keeps that hash. A password that has appeared in a known data breach is refused
([how the check works](/docs/sign-in-security#passwords-seen-in-data-breaches)). Mail apps use
[app passwords](/docs/clients/app-passwords) instead.

The panel stays open, so you can add several in a row. The mailbox shows **Setting up** in the list for a moment,
then **Active**.

Choose a mailbox's address in the list to open its details panel. **Edit name** under **Display name** on its
**Overview** changes the name people see on its mail (the person can also change their sender name in the webmail's
settings). **Overview** shows who it belongs to and how much
storage it uses; **Storage** and **Access** are where you change them. The ⋮ menu beside each address has the same actions,
and you can tick several mailboxes to suspend, resume or delete them together.

## Give people access to a mailbox

On the Plus and Supporter plans, owners and User Administrators can give people in the account access to a mailbox
that isn't theirs: a shared mailbox like `support@`, or a colleague's mailbox while they're away. Open the mailbox and
choose its **Delegation** tab (or **Delegation** in the ⋮ menu beside its address). There are three separate lists:

- **Read and manage**: open the mailbox and read, move and delete mail in all its folders. This doesn't include
  sending.
- **Send as**: send mail from the mailbox's address. Recipients see only the mailbox, not who sent it.
- **Send on behalf**: send mail from the mailbox that also names who sent it, shown as "Name on behalf of" the
  mailbox.

Give someone any combination. If they have both **Send as** and **Send on behalf**, **Send as** is used.

To add people:

1. Choose **Add people** under the list.
2. Tick each person. Everyone in the account is offered except the mailbox's own person and those already on the list.
3. Choose **Add**. If you haven't confirmed it's you in the last few minutes, the console asks for your console password
   or a code first.

To take access away, choose **Remove** beside the person. Each list changes on its own, so removing someone from
**Send as** leaves their **Read and manage** as it was. Changes can take up to a minute to apply.

- A mailbox can have up to <Value of="delegatesPerMailbox" /> people with access, and one person can have access to up
  to <Value of="delegatedMailboxesPerPerson" /> mailboxes.
- Only an owner can give or take away access to a mailbox that belongs to an owner or an admin.
- A person's **Mailboxes they can open** on [People and roles](/docs/people-and-roles#mailboxes-someone-can-open)
  lists every mailbox they have access to.
- Auditors can see the lists but can't change them. On the Free plan the tab says delegation isn't included.
- Every change is recorded in the [audit log](/docs/audit-log).
- Opening a mailbox you've been given access to, and sending from it, comes to the [webmail](/docs/webmail) next. Until
  then, the lists record who should have access.

## Convert to a shared mailbox

A person's mailbox can become a shared mailbox, for example when they leave or the address becomes a team's. Open the
mailbox, choose its **Access** tab (or **Convert to shared mailbox…** in the ⋮ menu beside its address), then
**Convert to shared mailbox…**:

1. Read what happens: the person can't sign in to it any more, in the webmail or mail apps, because its password is
   replaced with one nobody is shown and its [app passwords](/docs/clients/app-passwords) stop working. Webmail
   sessions open on it end within a minute. Its mail, address and aliases stay, and the person stays in the account.
2. Optionally, under **Give full access to**, tick the people who should have **Read and manage**, and **Also let them
   send as this mailbox** to give them **Send as** too. (Shown to owners and User Administrators on Plus and Supporter.)
3. Choose **Convert to shared mailbox**. The console may ask you to confirm it's you first.

Its **Overview** then says it belongs to nobody. To make it someone's mailbox again, use
[Make this a user](#make-a-shared-mailbox-a-user). Only an owner can convert an owner's or an admin's mailbox, and a
mailbox that is still being set up or is being deleted can't be converted.

## Storage used

The **Storage** column shows how much each mailbox holds out of its quota, with a bar. When a mailbox reaches 80% of
its quota the bar turns amber and says **Getting full**; from 95% it turns red and says **Almost full**, and **Full**
once there's no room left. A full mailbox stops accepting new mail until something is deleted or its storage is
raised. Choose the **Storage** column heading to sort mailboxes by how much they use.

- Storage is measured every ten minutes or so, so a message that just arrived may not be counted yet. The mailbox's
  **Storage** tab says when it was last measured. A new mailbox shows **—** until its first measurement.
- Only sizes are read. Nothing about your messages is looked at to measure them.
- The console's home page shows the total your account uses across all its mailboxes.

## Account storage

Storage belongs to the account, not to each mailbox: <Value of="freeStorage" /> on Free and <Value of="plusStorage" />
shared by the whole account on Plus and Supporter (see [what each plan
includes](/docs/free-address#what-each-plan-includes)). Each mailbox gets a share, and the shares never add up to more
than the account has. The top of the **Mailboxes** page shows how much the account uses, how much is assigned to
mailboxes and how much is unassigned, with a bar that turns amber at 80% and red at 95%.

- A new mailbox starts with an even share of what's unassigned. When everything is assigned, adding a mailbox says so:
  lower another mailbox's storage first, or add more storage once billing opens.
- More storage comes in blocks of +<Value of="storageBlock" /> for <Value of="storageBlockPrice" /> a year, with
  billing. Nothing is charged automatically.
- A full mailbox refuses new mail with a "mailbox full" bounce. Nothing is deleted.

## Change storage

Open the mailbox, choose the **Storage** tab and change **Storage (GB)** under **Change storage**. The tab shows the
mailbox's share and how much of the account's storage is unassigned. You can choose from what the mailbox already
holds (rounded up to the next GB) up to its current share plus everything unassigned. The new quota applies within a
minute.

When storage became shared per account, accounts whose mailboxes were given more than the account has were brought
within it: each mailbox's share went down in proportion, but never below the mail it already holds (rounded up to the
next GB), so it keeps receiving mail. Nothing was deleted. If an account's mail alone is more than its storage, the
**Mailboxes** page says the shares are above the account's storage.

## How many mailboxes

Plus and Supporter include <Value of="includedMailboxes" /> mailboxes per account. More mailboxes come with billing,
which isn't open yet, at <Value of="extraMailboxPrice" /> a year each. Free includes one address at our domain.

## Set a new password

When someone forgets their mailbox password, or a mailbox passes to someone new, open the mailbox, choose the
**Password** tab (or **Set password**), type or generate a new password and choose **Save new password**. If you
haven't confirmed it's you in the last few minutes, the console asks for your console password or a code first.

- The new password works within a minute. The old one stops working. Until it does, the mailbox can't change its own
  password from the webmail.
- If the new password couldn't be applied, the **Password** tab says so and the old password still works: set it
  again.
- Everyone signed in to the webmail with that mailbox is signed out. App passwords keep working. A mail app already
  connected with the old password (IMAP) stays connected until it disconnects.
- The person the mailbox belongs to gets an email saying its password was changed (never the password itself), and
  the change appears in the account's audit log.
- Staff never set a mailbox password while helping you in your account.

## Suspend a mailbox

Open the mailbox, choose the **Access** tab and then **Suspend mailbox**. Within a minute:

- Nobody can sign in to it: webmail, mail apps (IMAP, POP3 and SMTP) and app passwords are all refused. A mail app
  that is already connected can stay connected until it disconnects, but receives no new mail.
- Mail sent to it or to its aliases isn't delivered; the sender gets a bounce.
- Nothing in it is deleted. Messages, folders, settings and app passwords are all kept.
- If it was a domain's [catch-all](/docs/custom-domain/catch-all), the catch-all is turned off.

Choose **Resume mailbox** to turn it back on. It shows **Setting up** for a moment, then **Active**.

## Delete a mailbox

Open the mailbox, choose the **Access** tab, then **Delete mailbox…**, and confirm. You can restore a deleted mailbox
for <Value of="mailboxDeleteGraceDays" /> days:

- Straight away it's suspended (see above), and its aliases stop receiving mail too.
- Its address, and its aliases' addresses, stay reserved, so nobody else can take them.
- The list shows it as **Deleting**, with the date until which you can restore it.

After that date the mailbox, its aliases and all of its mail are permanently deleted, and the addresses are free
again. This can't be undone.

### Restore a deleted mailbox

Before that date, open the mailbox (or choose **Restore…** in its ⋮ menu) and choose **Restore mailbox**. It comes back with its aliases and all of its mail,
and shows **Active** again within a minute (even if it was suspended before you deleted it). A mailbox can't be
restored if its domain is no longer verified in your account.

## Who can manage mailboxes

Owners and User Admins can create, suspend, delete and restore mailboxes, change their storage, set a new password,
convert a person's mailbox to a shared mailbox and choose who has access to a mailbox. Only an owner can suspend,
delete, restore, convert or set the password of a mailbox that belongs to an owner or an admin, or change who has
access to it. Domain Admins and Auditors can see mailboxes; Auditors can
also see who has access to them.

If a suspension or deletion couldn't be applied on the mail server, the mailbox shows **Not applied** and its page
offers **Retry**.

A domain can't be removed while mailboxes use it, including deleted mailboxes that can still be restored.
