A mailbox is an address with its own storage, like `jane@example.org`. Every account can have an address at our
domain, <Value of="productDomain" /> (see [Your free address](/docs/free-address)). On the Plus and Supporter plans
you can also create mailboxes on any domain you've connected and verified (see
[Use your own domain](/docs/custom-domain)).

## Create a mailbox

1. In the console, open **Mailboxes** and choose **Add a mailbox** (or **New mailbox** at the top of the
   navigation). A panel opens on the right.
2. Type the part before the @ and choose the domain. Letters, numbers, dots, hyphens and underscores work;
   `+` is reserved, because `you+anything@` already reaches you. Our domain is in the list too, marked **free
   address**, while your plan has room for one; names there follow [their own rules](/docs/free-address#choosing-a-name).
3. Choose who it **belongs to**, or leave it for later.
4. Set its **storage** in whole GB, up to your plan's storage per mailbox.
5. Set its **password**: type one, or choose **Generate a strong password** and copy it. A generated password is
   shown only until you create the mailbox. Give it to the person safely; they sign in to the
   [webmail](/docs/webmail) with it and can change it there.

Mailbox passwords need 12 to 128 characters (a passphrase works well), no spaces at the start or end, and can't be
the mailbox's address or start with `app_`. We never store a mailbox password: it's turned into a one-way hash right
away, and only the mail server keeps that hash. Mail apps use [app passwords](/docs/clients/app-passwords) instead.

The panel stays open, so you can add several in a row. The mailbox shows **Setting up** in the list for a moment,
then **Active**.

Choose a mailbox's address in the list to open its details panel. **Overview** shows who it belongs to and its
storage; **Storage** and **Access** are where you change them. The ⋮ menu beside each address has the same actions,
and you can tick several mailboxes to suspend, resume or delete them together.

## Change storage

Open the mailbox, choose the **Storage** tab and change **Storage (GB)**. The new quota applies within a minute. Lowering it below what the mailbox
already holds doesn't delete anything, but the mailbox stops accepting new mail until there's room again.

## Set a new password

When someone forgets their mailbox password, or a mailbox passes to someone new, open the mailbox, choose the
**Password** tab (or **Set password**), type or generate a new password and choose **Save new password**. If you
haven't confirmed it's you in the last few minutes, the console asks for your console password or a code first.

- The new password works within a minute. The old one stops working.
- Everyone signed in to the webmail with that mailbox is signed out. App passwords keep working.
- The person the mailbox belongs to gets an email saying its password was changed (never the password itself), and
  the change appears in the account's audit log.
- Staff never set a mailbox password while helping you in your account.

## Suspend a mailbox

Open the mailbox, choose the **Access** tab and then **Suspend mailbox**. Within a minute:

- Nobody can sign in to it: webmail, mail apps (IMAP, POP3 and SMTP) and app passwords are all refused. A mail app
  that is already connected can stay connected until it disconnects, but receives no new mail.
- Mail sent to it or to its aliases isn't delivered; the sender gets a bounce.
- Nothing in it is deleted. Messages, folders, settings and app passwords are all kept.
- If it was a domain's [catch-all](/docs/custom-domain/catch-all), the catch-all is turned off.

Choose **Resume mailbox** to turn it back on. It shows **Setting up** for a moment, then **Active**.

## Delete a mailbox

Open the mailbox, choose the **Access** tab, then **Delete mailbox…**, and confirm. A deleted mailbox can be restored for

<Value of="mailboxDeleteGraceDays" /> days:

- Straight away it's suspended (see above), and its aliases stop receiving mail too.
- Its address, and its aliases' addresses, stay reserved, so nobody else can take them.
- The list shows it as **Deleting**, with the date until which you can restore it.

After that date the mailbox, its aliases and all of its mail are permanently deleted, and the addresses are free
again. This can't be undone.

### Restore a deleted mailbox

Before that date, open the mailbox (or choose **Restore…** in its ⋮ menu) and choose **Restore mailbox**. It comes back with its aliases and all of its mail,
and shows **Active** again within a minute (even if it was suspended before you deleted it). A mailbox can't be
restored if its domain is no longer verified in your account.

## Who can manage mailboxes

Owners and User Admins can create, suspend, delete and restore mailboxes, change their storage and set a new password.
Only an owner can suspend, delete, restore or set the password of a mailbox that belongs to an owner or an admin. Domain Admins and Auditors can see
mailboxes.

If a suspension or deletion couldn't be applied on the mail server, the mailbox shows **Not applied** and its page
offers **Retry**.

A domain can't be removed while mailboxes use it, including deleted mailboxes that can still be restored.
