<Callout title="Partly here">
  The [webmail](/docs/webmail) shows your folders with their unread counts, archives with one key and moves mail between
  folders, and [signatures](#signatures) are here. Creating, renaming and sharing folders, favourites, archive by year,
  search folders and rules are on their way; until then, folders you create in a mail app show in the webmail too.
</Callout>

## Folders

Every mailbox starts with Inbox, Drafts, Sent, Archive, Junk and Trash. You can add your own folders, put folders
inside folders, rename them and drag them around. Inbox, Junk and Trash can't be deleted.

- **Favourites:** pin the folders you use most to the top of the list.
- **Archive:** one click (or the `e` key) moves a message out of your inbox into Archive without deleting it. Mail
  apps that use IMAP see the same folder as their archive. Archive by year, so old mail lands in a folder like
  Archive / 2026, is coming.
- **Search folders:** save a search, such as "unread from family", and it appears as a folder that updates itself.
- **Rules:** "always move mail from this sender to this folder" runs on the server, so it works in every app.
- **Sharing:** share a folder with someone in your account, read-only or with full access.

Mail in Junk and Trash is cleaned up automatically after a while. Empty them yourself any time.

### Folders in mail apps

Mail apps that use IMAP see your folders. Hide a folder from mail apps with **Show in mail apps** in the folder
menu. POP3 only ever downloads the Inbox: that is how POP3 works. Use IMAP if you want folders on your phone or
desktop. Server settings are on the [mail app settings](/docs/clients/imap-smtp-settings) page.

## Signatures

Create signatures in the webmail under [Settings → Writing](/docs/webmail-settings#writing) and choose which one each
kind of message starts with.

- **Add signature** opens the editor: give it a name (up to <Value of="signatureNameChars" /> characters, different
  from your other signatures) and write it with the same toolbar as your messages — fonts, sizes, bold, italic,
  underline, strikethrough, colours, alignment, lists, indents and links.
- **Images:** choose **Insert image**, or paste or drop a picture into the editor. Images are kept inside the
  signature itself: PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP, up to <Value of="signatureImageSize" /> each and{" "}
  <Value of="signatureImages" /> per signature (a whole signature can be up to <Value of="signatureMaxSize" />
  ). Links to images on other websites are removed, because they would let that website see when and where your mail is
  read.
- Each signature in the list has **Edit**, **Delete** and, under **More options**, **Duplicate**. You can keep up to{" "}
  <Value of="signaturesPerMailbox" /> signatures in a mailbox, up to <Value of="signaturesMaxSize" /> together.
- **Default for new messages** and **Default for replies and forwards** choose the signature each kind of message
  starts with; either can be **(No signature)**. The first signature you add becomes both defaults. Deleting a
  default leaves that kind of message without a signature until you choose another.
- Signatures belong to the mailbox, so they work with every address you send from — your own, aliases and masked
  aliases. Changing **From** while you write keeps the signature you chose.
- **While you write:** the **Signature** button at the bottom of the message lists your signatures and **(No
  signature)**; choosing one replaces only the signature, not the rest of your message. In plain text mode the
  signature is added as text after a `-- ` line.

If you used the webmail before named signatures, your signature is kept: it appears in the list as **Signature**, the
default for new messages and for replies.

On paid plans, an account owner will be able to set **signature templates** that fill in each person's name and job
title, and a **disclaimer** added to outgoing mail. Both are on their way.

### Signatures in mail apps

Desktop and phone mail apps keep their own signatures, so set your signature in the app as well. Mail apps that use
JMAP can also see your default signature for new messages; they get a shorter copy without images when a signature
is too large for them.
