The webmail is AnhourMail in your browser, at <Value of="webmailHost" />. It's a separate address from the console
(<Value of="appHost" />), so signing in to one doesn't sign you in to the other.

## Open webmail from the console

If you're signed in to the console, choose **Open webmail** in the sidebar, your menu or on the home page. Your own
mailbox opens straight away, without its password. If you have more than one mailbox, pick the one to open. If you
didn't sign in to the console in the last few minutes, you're asked to confirm it's you first (your console password
or a code), as when you make an app password.

- It only opens mailboxes that are **yours** (assigned to you), never someone else's or a shared mailbox.
- The link works once, for <Value of="webmailHandoffSeconds" /> seconds, and only in the browser you started from. If you copy it to another
  browser or open it again, you're asked to start over from the console.
- It isn't available while Anhourtec support has your account open, for Auditors, while a mailbox is suspended or
  being deleted, or before you've chosen your own password for a new mailbox. In those cases you're offered the
  ordinary sign-in below.
- No password is involved: the console never sees or passes along your mailbox password, and the one-time key used
  to open your mailbox is deleted straight away. Signing out of the webmail, or changing the
  mailbox password, ends the session as usual. Signing out of the console (or signing out everywhere) ends a webmail
  session opened this way too.

## Sign in

1. Go to <Value of="webmailHost" />. The sign-in page never fills in an address from a link, so type it yourself (or
   let your password manager).
2. Enter your **mailbox address** and your **mailbox password** — the password of the mailbox itself, not an app
   password.
3. Choose **Sign in**.

If the address or password is wrong you'll see "Mailbox address or password is wrong." We don't say which one, so nobody
can use the form to find out which addresses exist. After several wrong tries in a row, wait a few minutes before
trying again.

Your password is only used to sign in. We don't store it: the mail server checks it and gives your browser session
a key that stays on our servers, never in the browser. Your mailbox password is the one set when the mailbox was
created; change it in [Settings → Account](/docs/webmail-settings#account). Changing it keeps you signed in where you
changed it and signs the webmail out everywhere else; when an admin sets a new one, the webmail signs out everywhere.
Forgot it? Ask an owner or admin of your account to [set a new one](/docs/mailboxes#set-a-new-password).

If an admin set your password and asked you to change it, the webmail shows **Choose your own password** before
your mail. Enter the **password you were given** and a new one twice (or choose **Generate a strong password**), then
**Save and open my mail**. Mail apps and app passwords aren't affected. If the change takes a moment to apply, you're
asked to sign in again with the new password in a minute.

If the webmail says a mailbox can't be used, the message says why:

- **The mailbox is suspended** by your account's admin (see [Mailboxes](/docs/mailboxes)) or because Anhourtec
  [suspended the account](/docs/account-suspended). Its mail is kept. Ask your admin to resume it, or contact
  <Value of="supportEmail" /> if Anhourtec suspended it.
- **Your sign-in is suspended:** Anhourtec suspended you, so none of your mailboxes can be used. Contact
  <Value of="supportEmail" /> if you think this is a mistake.
- **The mailbox is being deleted:** an admin can still restore it for a while.
- **The mailbox no longer exists.**

When this happens while you're signed in, the webmail signs you out and shows the reason. A browser where you have
signed in to that mailbox before also shows it when you sign in again. On a browser you haven't used for it, you see
"Mailbox address or password is wrong." instead, so nobody can use the sign-in form to find out which mailboxes are
suspended.

## Several mailboxes

If you use more than one mailbox, open the menu (⋮) at the top of the sidebar and choose **Add another mailbox**, then
sign in to it with its own address and password. Each mailbox gets a square tile at the top of the sidebar; choose a
tile to switch. You can have up to five open at once. **Sign out** signs out of all of them.

## Mailboxes shared with you

When an owner or admin gives you access to another mailbox in your account (see
[Give people access to a mailbox](/docs/mailboxes#give-people-access-to-a-mailbox)), it shows up in your webmail by
itself. You don't need its password or a second sign-in: your own sign-in is enough. Changes can take up to a minute
to show.

- **Shared with you.** Mailboxes you have **Read and manage** on are listed under **Shared with you** in the sidebar
  and in the menu (⋮) at the top of it. Choose one to open it: a line across the top says "You're viewing" that
  mailbox, and **Back to your mailbox** returns to yours. You can read, move, flag and delete its mail in all its
  folders.
- **Open another mailbox.** In the menu (⋮) at the top of the sidebar, choose **Open another mailbox**, type the
  mailbox's address and choose **Open**. It opens in a new tab at its own address (`/mailbox/` followed by the address),
  which shows that mailbox only; your other tabs stay on your own mailbox. You can bookmark it: access is checked
  again each time it opens. If you follow a link to it from another website, the page asks you to choose **Open**
  first. If you don't have access, or the address doesn't exist, the tab says "You don't have
  access to this mailbox" and links back to your own.
- **What stays with your own mailbox.** Writing and sending, replies, contacts, blocking a sender, settings and
  signatures always belong to your own mailbox, so they aren't offered while a shared one is shown. To send from a
  shared mailbox, write from your own (see [Sending as or on behalf of a mailbox](#sending-as-or-on-behalf-of-a-mailbox)).
- **When access ends.** If your access is taken away, the mailbox leaves **Shared with you** and an open tab of it says
  you don't have access any more.
- Mailboxes shared with you open in the webmail only, not in mail apps. You can have up to
  <Value of="delegatedMailboxesPerPerson" /> of them open at once.

## Reading mail

- On a computer you see your folders, the list of conversations and the open message side by side. On a phone you
  see one at a time: choose a conversation to open it, and the back arrow to return to the list. The sidebar folds
  into a narrow rail with the button at the top of the list, and is a drawer on phones.
- The list shows the newest first, with how long ago each arrived ("24 minutes ago", "1 day ago", then the date). A
  blue dot after the sender means unread; the number beside a sender is how many messages the conversation holds.
- Opening a conversation marks it read. Use the arrows at the top of the message to go to the previous or next one.
- A conversation shows every message in it, oldest first, under the subject and the folders it's in. The newest
  message and any unread ones are open; earlier ones are folded to one line — choose one to open it, or use
  **Expand all** / **Collapse all**. Choose the recipients under the sender's name (with the small arrow) to see the
  message's details, including who sent and signed it (**mailed-by** and **signed-by**) when our mail server checked
  it.
- The list checks for new mail every half minute while the webmail is open. When a reply arrives in the conversation
  you're reading, it appears there too, without closing it; **Refresh** above the list updates both straight away.
- The counts beside your folders (unread mail, and the number of drafts) update as you read, move, delete, send or
  discard mail, and within half a minute when you do it in another app or on another device.
- Plain-text messages keep their line breaks, and links and addresses in them work. Earlier messages quoted in a
  reply are folded away behind **…** — choose it to show them; signatures are shown lighter.
- Attachments are listed under the sender, with their size. Pictures show a small preview; choose one to see it
  bigger. Forwarded messages open in place. Everything else downloads when you choose it; files are never opened in
  the webmail itself.
- Calendar invitations show as a card with the time, place and organiser. Replying to invitations is coming soon.
- Each message has its own ⋮ menu: **Reply**, **Reply all**, **Forward**, **Delete**, **Mark unread from here**,
  **Block** the sender, **Report spam** (or **Not spam**), **Report phishing**, **Print**, **Download message** (an
  `.eml` file) and **Show original**.
- **Show original** opens a new tab with the message's ID, when it was created and how long it took to arrive, who it
  was from and to, whether it passed the **SPF**, **DKIM** and **DMARC** checks, and its full source. Use
  **Download original** or **Copy to clipboard** to share it, for example with support.
- Very long messages show the first part with **View entire message**.
- **Priority** in the sidebar collects every message you've flagged, in any folder.
- **Pinned** conversations stay at the top of their folder, in their own section. Use the pin button on a message.

## Writing mail

- Choose **New email** in the sidebar (or press `c`); on a phone or tablet it floats at the bottom of the list.
  Type addresses in **To**; press Enter or a comma after each one, or pick from the suggestions (your
  [contacts](/docs/contacts) and people you've written to, the ones you email most first). **Cc Bcc** adds those fields. People you send to are saved to **Other contacts** automatically
  (you can turn this off).
- If you have aliases or masked aliases, choose which address to send from in **From**.
- New messages, replies and forwards start with your default [signature](/docs/folders-and-signatures#signatures)
  for that kind of message. The **Signature** button at the bottom switches to another one or removes it.
- Attach files with the paperclip (up to <Value of="attachmentPerFile" /> each, <Value of="attachmentPerMessage" /> per
  message).
- Your message is saved to **Drafts** a couple of seconds after you stop typing. Close it and carry on later from the
  Drafts folder; the bin button discards it.
- To reply, use the **Reply to …** box under a message, or the reply button. **Reply all**, **Forward** and more are
  in the ⋮ menu. Replies quote the original message and stay in the same conversation.
- **Undo send:** after you choose **Send**, you have a few seconds to choose **Undo** — the message goes back to your
  drafts and nothing is delivered.
- You can send to up to <Value of="recipientsPerMessage" /> people per message, and each person counts toward your
  [sending limits](/docs/sending-limits).

### Sending as or on behalf of a mailbox

If you've been given **Send as** or **Send on behalf** on a mailbox (see
[Give people access to a mailbox](/docs/mailboxes#give-people-access-to-a-mailbox)), **From** lists it under
**Shared with you**, after your own addresses:

- **The mailbox's address** sends as the mailbox. Recipients see only the mailbox, not you.
- **"You on behalf of the mailbox"** sends from the mailbox and names you as the sender. Recipients see "You on
  behalf of" the mailbox.
- If you have both, only **send as** is offered.

Write the message from your own mailbox as usual; your draft stays in your Drafts. When you send, it goes from the
shared mailbox, and its copy is kept in **that mailbox's** Sent folder, so everyone with access sees what was sent.
**Undo** works the same way. It counts toward the shared mailbox's sending limits as well as yours. If your access
was taken away in the meantime, the message isn't sent.

Rarely, the connection drops just as a message is sent and we can't tell whether it went. You'll see "We couldn't
confirm whether this was sent. Check Sent before sending it again." Look in the shared mailbox's Sent folder before
you try again.

When you read a message sent on behalf of a mailbox, the line under the sender says "On behalf of" the mailbox,
"sent by" the person who sent it. That comes from the message itself, like the rest of its header details.

## Rich text and HTML email

Messages are written in rich text. The toolbar above the message has:

- **Undo** and **Redo**;
- **Font** (sans serif, serif or monospace) and **Text size** (small, normal, large or huge);
- bold, italic, underline and strikethrough;
- **Text colour** and **Highlight**;
- **Align** (left, centre or right), bulleted and numbered lists, and **Decrease indent** / **Increase indent**;
- links, quotes, **Clear formatting** and **Insert HTML**.

The usual shortcuts work too: `Ctrl`/`⌘` + `B`, `I` and `U`, `Ctrl`/`⌘` + `K` for a link, and `Ctrl`/`⌘` + `Z` to
undo. On a phone, swipe the toolbar sideways to see the rest of it. Fonts are the reader's own sans-serif, serif or
monospace font, so nothing is downloaded when your message is read.

To send text only, open **More options** (the ⋯ button next to the paperclip) and choose **Plain text mode**. If your
message has formatting, you're asked first: switching removes it and keeps your text; **Cancel** leaves the message
as it is. In plain text, the same menu has **Switch to rich text**. A draft you wrote in plain text reopens in plain
text.

Rich messages are sent with a plain-text copy alongside, so mail apps that don't show formatting still show your
words: each line stays a line, list items start with `-` or their number, and indented text isn't marked as a
quote. Replies and forwards quote the original with its formatting. A reply's quote is marked as one, so mail apps
(the webmail included) fold it away, and in the plain-text copy the quoted lines start with `>` under the "On …
wrote:" line.

**Pasting:** formatted text you paste keeps its safe formatting (headings, lists, tables, links, colours). Anything
else is removed, including the toolbars and buttons some pages and browser extensions add.

**Insert HTML** (up to <Value of="htmlMaxSize" />): the code button in the toolbar opens **Insert HTML**. Paste
HTML or upload an `.html` file. You see a preview of exactly what will be sent, a list of anything that was removed,
and a warning if it loads images from another server. Choose **Insert** to add it where your cursor was. Images from
another server show as a placeholder in the preview and while you write — nothing is loaded from that server on your
device — and are sent as they are.

### What is removed from HTML you send

Mail you send is shown by other people's mail apps, so we only send HTML that is safe for them to open. Every
message is cleaned in your browser while you write and again on our servers before it is saved, so nothing can skip
the check.

- **Kept:** paragraphs and line breaks, headings, bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, lists, quotes, code,
  tables, horizontal rules, images and links, and inline styles for colours, fonts, spacing, borders, alignment and
  sizes.
- **Removed:** scripts and event handlers, forms and their fields and buttons, embedded frames, objects and plugins,
  SVG, style sheets (`<style>` and `<link>`; inline styles are kept), and elements we don't recognise (their text
  is kept).
- **Links** must start with `https://`, `http://`, `mailto:` or `tel:`, and always open in a new tab.
- **Images** must be `https://` addresses or pictures embedded in the message (PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP, up to
  <Value of="htmlInlineImage" /> each).
- **Styles** that could cover the page or load code (such as fixed positioning, `expression()`, `var()` or
  `@import`) are removed; background images keep only `https://` addresses. Colours, fonts, sizes, alignment,
  spacing and strikethrough are kept only as plain values (for example `#2563eb`, `serif`, `large`, `center` or
  `40px`); anything else in them, such as a function or a negative margin, is removed.

<Callout type="warn">
  Images loaded from another server can tell whoever runs that server when and where your message was opened. Insert
  HTML warns you when the HTML has them; embed pictures in the message instead if you'd rather not.
</Callout>

## Organising mail

- **Archive** keeps a conversation but takes it out of the Inbox. **Delete** moves it to Trash; in Trash (and Junk)
  you can delete forever, after a confirmation. Both Trash and Junk have **Empty** in their ⋯ menu.
- **Restore** in Trash puts a conversation back where it belongs: messages you sent go to Sent, everything else to
  the Inbox.
- **Report spam** moves a conversation to Junk and helps the filters learn; **Not spam** moves it back. **Report
  phishing** does the same for mail that tries to trick you into giving away a password or money.
- **Block** in a message's ⋮ menu adds the sender to your
  [blocked senders](/docs/webmail-settings#junk-and-blocked-senders) and moves the conversation to Junk.
- **Move to** in the ⋮ menu files a conversation in any folder.
- Archive, delete, junk, move and restore show a notice with **Undo** for a few seconds.
- **Select several:** hover over the square avatar and tick it (or press `x`); hold Shift to tick a range. The bar that
  appears acts on all of them.

## Search and filters

Type in **Search…** above the list (or press `/`) and press Enter. Search covers every folder except Trash and Junk
(search inside Trash or Junk while you are in them). You can narrow it with:

| Write             | To find                   |
| ----------------- | ------------------------- |
| `from:sam`        | messages from someone     |
| `to:jordan@…`     | messages to someone       |
| `subject:"cabin"` | words in the subject      |
| `has:attachment`  | messages with attachments |
| `is:unread`       | unread messages           |
| `is:flagged`      | messages in Priority      |

The filter button beside the title shows only unread, Priority, or messages with attachments.

## Settings

Theme, density, the reading pane, signatures, automatic replies, forwarding and blocked senders are all in
**Settings** (at the bottom of the sidebar, or press `,`). See [Webmail settings](/docs/webmail-settings).

## Remote images are blocked until you load them

Many messages, especially newsletters, load their pictures from the sender's servers when you open them. That
tells the sender when you opened the message, roughly where you are, and which device you use — the same trick
tracking pixels rely on.

So the webmail **doesn't load remote images by itself**. You'll see a striped placeholder where an image would be and
a note saying how many were blocked. Tiny tracking pixels are removed entirely and never load. Pictures that are part
of the message itself (sent inside it) always show.

### Loading images through our privacy proxy

Choose **Load images** on the note to see the pictures. They're fetched by our servers, not your browser, and then
shown in the message, so the sender's server sees our request instead of you: never your IP address, your browser,
your cookies or the page you're on.

- When we can confirm who sent the message, **Load images** asks how: **Just this once**, or **Always for** the
  sender's address, which loads their images by themselves from then on. Press Escape to change your mind.
  After the images load, **Stop loading from this sender** undoes "always" (and **Always load from this sender**
  remembers a sender you loaded once).
- When we can't confirm the message really comes from the sender's domain (it didn't pass the domain's sender
  checks), **Load images** loads them this once and the note says so: we'll ask again next time, because a message
  that only pretends to be from someone mustn't be able to tell when you open it.
- In **Settings → Reading → Remote images** you can also load images from people in your contacts, or always.
- Images load by themselves for a remembered sender or a contact only when the message really comes from that
  sender's domain. A message that only pretends to be from them asks first.
- Each image can be up to <Value of="remoteImageSize" />, and up to <Value of="remoteImagesPerMessage" /> images load
  per message. Pictures in other formats (such as SVG) and images used as backgrounds stay blocked.
- Loading an image still tells the sender that _someone_ opened the message through our proxy. If that matters, leave
  them blocked.

## How messages are kept safe

Every message is cleaned on our servers before you see it: scripts, forms, embedded frames and anything that
could run code or reach out to the internet are removed. The cleaned message is then shown in an isolated frame
that can't run code or reach the rest of the page. Links open in a new tab and don't tell the site where you came
from.
